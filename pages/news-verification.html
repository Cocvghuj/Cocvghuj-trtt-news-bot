How we verify news before publishing

Our six practical checks for travel and aviation updates. We aim to make the evidence behind a story clear—and to be honest when we cannot confirm something.

Our six verification rules

  1. 01 · Start with the record

    Look for original, authoritative sources

    For airline, airport, safety, schedule, or passenger-rights claims, we seek the relevant regulator, public authority, airline, airport, or service provider’s own published information first.

  2. 02 · Corroborate

    Check important claims against more than one source

    When a claim is significant, we look for independent corroboration where available. Repeated copies of the same original report do not count as separate confirmation.

  3. 03 · Check the details

    Verify dates, place, and context

    We check when information was published and when it applies, along with locations, route details, units, and relevant conditions. Old or out-of-context information should not be presented as a current update.

  4. 04 · Trace each claim

    Keep evidence close to the statement

    We link factual claims to the sources that support them, distinguish confirmed information from estimates or opinion, and do not describe a source as proof of something it does not say.

  5. 05 · Be transparent

    Disclose uncertainty and commercial interests

    We avoid unsupported certainty. If a link may earn us a commission, we disclose that relationship; commercial pages are used for their own offers or policies, not passed off as independent authority.

  6. 06 · Correct and clarify

    Fix errors when we find them

    If a published detail is wrong or changes, we review it and update or clarify the page when appropriate. If we cannot verify a claim, we say “তথ্য পাইনি” (“we couldn’t find verified information”) rather than guess or present it as fact.

The rule that matters most

যাচাই না হলে — “তথ্য পাইনি”

No reliable confirmation means no invented answer. We may hold a story, qualify it, or explain what remains unknown.
