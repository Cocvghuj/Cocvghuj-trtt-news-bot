EUROPEAN PASSENGER RIGHTS · EC 261/2004

Flight delayed or cancelled?

You may have rights to care, a refund or re-routing — and in some situations, financial compensation. The rules depend on your route, arrival delay, the reason for the disruption and when you were told about a cancellation.

Possible compensation amounts

These are standard compensation bands, not a promise that every disrupted flight qualifies.

€250Flights up to 1,500 km
€400Other flights up to 3,500 km, and qualifying intra-EU flights over 1,500 km
€600Qualifying flights over 3,500 km

When might you qualify?

  • Long arrival delay: reaching your final destination at least 3 hours late may qualify, unless the airline proves extraordinary circumstances. For some long-haul journeys, the threshold is 4 hours.
  • Cancellation: compensation may be due when you were informed less than 14 days before departure, subject to the rerouting rules and exceptions.
  • Denied boarding: involuntary denied boarding can create compensation and assistance rights when you met the check-in and travel-document requirements.

EU passenger-rights rules generally cover flights within the EU, flights departing the EU, and certain flights arriving in the EU on an EU airline. Extraordinary circumstances can affect compensation, but do not automatically remove every right to care or re-routing.

Keep your evidence

  • Booking confirmation and booking reference
  • Boarding pass or e-ticket
  • Airline messages showing the reason and timing of disruption
  • Actual arrival time at your final destination
  • Receipts for reasonable expenses you had to pay

What to do next

  1. Write to the operating airline and explain what happened.
  2. Ask for the reason for the delay or cancellation and keep the reply.
  3. If the airline does not resolve it, consult the national enforcement body for the country involved.
