1. European Passenger Rights: The Power of Regulation (EC) No 261/2004

Navigating modern European air transit requires understanding your legal safeguards. European Union Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 is one of the world's most robust passenger protection statutes, ensuring financial compensation and duty of care when flights suffer substantial delays, short-notice cancellations, or involuntary denied boarding.

Crucially, this legal framework applies to all flights departing from any European Union airport (regardless of carrier nationality) and flights arriving into the EU on EU-licensed operating airlines (such as ITA Airways, Ryanair, Wizz Air, and easyJet). Unlike travel insurance, which often involves complicated deductibles, EC 261/2004 compensation is a statutory legal obligation paid directly to the passenger.

2. Statutory Compensation Tiers: €250, €400, and €600

The compensation amount under EC 261/2004 is non-negotiable and structured strictly by flight distance, not ticket purchase price. Even on a budget flight costing €19, eligible passengers are legally entitled to standard compensation:

Tier 1 • Under 1,500 km
€250

Short-haul flights (e.g., Cagliari to Rome, Cagliari to Bergamo, Milan to Sardinia). Delay ≥ 3 hours.

Tier 2 • 1,500 to 3,500 km
€400

Medium-haul intra-EU flights and connecting trans-continental routes across Europe.

Tier 3 • Over 3,500 km
€600

Long-haul international flights departing EU gateways to Asia, Americas, or the Middle East.

3. The Statutory "Duty of Care" During Delays

When delays surpass 2 hours on short flights or 3 hours on longer journeys, airlines are legally required to provide immediate assistance:

  • Food & Refreshment Vouchers: Free meal and beverage vouchers proportionate to waiting duration.
  • Communications Access: Two free phone calls, faxes, or email communications.
  • Hotel Accommodation: Free overnight accommodation and dedicated airport-to-hotel transfers whenever departure is postponed to the next day.

Crucial Rule on "Extraordinary Circumstances": Airlines frequently blame technical faults as "extraordinary." However, European Court of Justice rulings firmly state that routine mechanical failures and crew shortages do NOT exempt airlines from compensation. Genuine extraordinary circumstances are restricted to severe freak weather closures, airport air traffic control strikes, or geopolitical emergencies.

4. Island Logistics: Sanluri to Cagliari Elmas Airport (CAG)

For travelers exploring Sardinia, connecting smoothly between regional towns and international terminals is essential. The town of Sanluri, situated in the heart of Medio Campidano, serves as an optimal transit hub for southern Sardinia:

  • By Road (SS131 Carlo Felice): Driving south along Strada Statale 131 takes approximately 30 to 35 minutes directly into Cagliari Elmas Airport departures concourse.
  • By Rail (Trenitalia): Trains operating between Sanluri Stato and Elmas Aeroporto deliver passengers directly to the terminal without traffic delays.
  • Baggage Discipline: For low-cost travelers flying Ryanair or Wizz Air from CAG, ensuring cabin bag measurements conform strictly to 40x20x25cm prevents unexpected gate penalties of €40 to €70.
