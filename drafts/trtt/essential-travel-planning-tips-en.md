# Thinking Room: A Practical Travel Planning Workspace Built Around People

A useful travel website should do more than publish articles. It should help people understand their options, ask better questions, compare the details that matter and make their own informed decisions. That is the idea behind the Thinking Room concept being developed for TRTT Voyage.

Thinking Room is planned as a clear starting point for travel-related tools, guides and services. Instead of making visitors search through a crowded menu, the goal is to organize useful routes in one place and make the next step easier to understand. The concept is still being developed; features should be described as available only after they have been tested and confirmed to work.

## What Thinking Room is intended to do

A traveller may need to compare a flight, plan a ground transfer, check mobile connectivity, research a tour or find useful travel products. These are related needs, but they are not the same task. A well-organized menu can guide visitors to the right starting point without promising that every service is sold or operated directly by TRTT Voyage.

The planned menu brings the main travel journeys into a more understandable structure:

- **Flight:** research flight options and understand the details to compare before booking.
- **Ride:** plan the journey between airports, stations, accommodation and destinations.
- **eSIM:** understand mobile data options and check device compatibility before travelling.
- **Tour:** research activities, local experiences and practical visit information.
- **Shop:** explore travel-related products and accessories with clear product information.
- **Night:** find relevant evening or nightlife planning information where it is useful and appropriate.

The purpose is guidance and navigation. Visitors should always be able to identify the actual provider, read its terms and complete a booking or purchase through the provider's official process. TRTT Voyage should not imply that it directly operates an airline, transport company, tour operator or shop unless that is genuinely the case.

## Why a clear travel menu helps visitors

Travel planning often involves several websites, different policies and information that changes over time. A visitor can lose time when a menu is confusing or when a page does not explain what happens after a link is selected.

A useful menu should answer three simple questions:

1. **Where should I start?** The labels should describe the task in familiar language.
2. **What will happen next?** A link should lead to the service or explanation it promises.
3. **Who is responsible?** The visitor should know whether information comes from TRTT Voyage or an external provider.

The design should work on a mobile phone as well as a desktop. Buttons and links need readable labels, enough space to tap, good contrast and a clear way to return to the main menu. Decorative effects should never make navigation harder or hide important information.

## The Thinking Room approach to better AI prompts

Thinking Room is also connected to a wider experiment: improving the way people give instructions to AI tools such as ChatGPT.

AI assistants can misunderstand vague requests, miss important restrictions or produce an answer that sounds confident but has not been verified. People can also give incomplete, contradictory or confusing prompts. Better instructions can reduce avoidable misunderstandings, but they do not remove the need for the AI system to be accurate, honest about uncertainty and respectful of the user's limits.

The work focuses on correcting common prompt mistakes and making instructions clearer: state the goal, provide the relevant context, identify what must not be changed, specify the expected format and ask for uncertainty to be explained rather than guessed. The ambition is modest but meaningful: even if the contribution represents only **0.00001 of a step** toward better prompting, it should help make AI assistance more useful to people.

This is a prompt-quality and workflow-improvement effort. It does not mean that an individual user is changing ChatGPT's underlying model or training it globally. Any AI-assisted information published by TRTT Voyage still needs human review, fact-checking and clear responsibility.

### A simple prompt structure travellers can use

A traveller can make an AI request more useful by including:

- **Goal:** what decision or task needs help?
- **Context:** destination, dates, number of travellers and relevant circumstances.
- **Constraints:** budget, baggage needs, accessibility, time limits or other requirements.
- **Output:** comparison table, checklist, itinerary or short explanation.
- **Verification:** ask which details may change and where to confirm them officially.

For example: “Help me compare flight options for a trip from Rome to London in September. I need one checked bag and prefer daytime arrivals. Explain the total-cost factors and list the details I must confirm with the airline. Do not invent live prices or availability.”

AI can help organize questions and compare information supplied to it. It should not be treated as the final authority for visa decisions, immigration eligibility, safety advice, live fares or provider policies. Confirm those details with the relevant government, airline or service provider.

## A practical 15-day development priority

A focused development period is more useful than adding many unfinished features. The following is a proposed priority order for the next 15 days, not a claim that the work has already been completed.

### Days 1–3: Make the menu understandable
Review the Thinking Room structure, labels and navigation flow. Confirm that every visible route has a clear purpose and that mobile visitors can open, use and close the menu reliably.

### Days 4–6: Check the travel journeys
Review the Flight, Ride, eSIM, Tour, Shop and Night routes. Identify which pages and providers are genuinely available, remove misleading promises and make the next step clear.

### Days 7–9: Improve the information visitors rely on
Prepare practical checklists and evergreen explanations. Separate general guidance from information that changes frequently, such as prices, schedules, baggage rules, entry requirements and provider terms.

### Days 10–12: Test prompts and visitor questions
Collect common examples of unclear travel prompts, rewrite them into specific instructions and test whether the resulting answers are easier to understand. Record errors and limitations instead of hiding them.

### Days 13–15: Review quality before expanding
Test mobile usability, links, page clarity, spelling, image relevance and the accuracy of claims. Fix the most important problems before adding more routes or publishing more pages.

## Travelpayouts and Amazon: a careful affiliate plan

TRTT Voyage is considering affiliate partnerships such as Travelpayouts for relevant travel services and Amazon Associates for suitable travel products. These are plans, not a statement that either programme has approved the site or that the site is currently earning commissions.

If an application is accepted and the relevant tools are configured, affiliate links should be used only where they genuinely help the reader. A flight-related link should support a useful flight-planning explanation; a product link should be accompanied by original context about who may need the item, what to compare and what limitations to check.

The site should not copy merchant descriptions and publish them as if they were original reviews. Where products are discussed, useful information may include practical selection criteria, compatibility, limitations and a transparent explanation of what has or has not been personally tested.

Any required affiliate disclosure must be clear and easy to notice before or near the relevant links. Commercial links should be qualified appropriately, such as with `rel="sponsored"` where the publishing platform allows it. Readers should never be led to believe that a recommendation is independent if a commission may be earned.

## How this guide follows a people-first publishing approach

Google's published Search guidance emphasizes helpful, reliable, people-first content. It does not promise rankings for a particular word count, keyword density or publishing schedule. The useful standard is whether a page answers a real visitor need with original value, clear writing and trustworthy information.

For TRTT Voyage, that means:

- Write for travellers first, not for a search engine checklist.
- Explain the subject fully enough for a reader to take the next sensible step.
- Use descriptive titles and headings that match the page's real subject.
- Avoid repeating keywords unnaturally or creating many near-identical pages.
- Do not invent first-hand experience, testing, qualifications, prices or provider relationships.
- Review time-sensitive details and show readers where to verify them.
- Use relevant images and describe them accurately.
- Make navigation, links and page content work for people using mobile devices.
- Use AI as an assistant for research and structure, not as a substitute for editorial responsibility.

## Final thoughts

Thinking Room should grow into a dependable starting point, not a collection of promises. The best first step is to make the existing routes clear, useful and reliable, then expand only when each new feature has been checked. A visitor should leave with a better understanding of the journey and the confidence to verify important decisions with the right provider.
