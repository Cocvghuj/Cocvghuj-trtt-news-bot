# A Traveller’s Front Door: Why I Am Building the Thinking Room Menu Before More Flight Posts
**FlightScoutPro field note | 10 November 2026**  
**Status: Draft for review — not published**

I have spent much of the last fifteen days working on something that may look small: a menu. While I could have written another flight update or prepared more pages for search, I decided to make the route through the project clearer first. I want a traveller to land on the site and understand what help is available, where to go next, and which links lead to an actual service.

This is a personal project note, not a live fare alert or a claim that every service is already fully operational.

## Why a menu is part of travel support

Travel planning is a chain of decisions. A flight is only one part of it. Before departure, a traveller may compare routes and baggage rules. At the airport, they may need a ride or mobile data. After a delay or cancellation, they may need to understand passenger rights. At the destination, they may need local transport, tours, accommodation or practical equipment.

I want the Thinking Room menu to make these routes easier to find. The eleven planned entries are Home, My Diary, Flight, Ride, eSIM, Tour, Shop, Night, Thinking Room, ROKSANA LOCKED and About MAC. The names are navigation labels, not promises that every service is already available. A locked destination must be clearly marked as locked until it is ready.

## Why Thinking Room belongs in the header

A floating control can cover content, overlap a booking link or become awkward on a phone. I want the Thinking Room launcher inside the existing header navigation, in the normal document flow, with static positioning rather than fixed positioning. The aim is simple: make the menu easy to find without making it obstruct the page.

The overlay must also be easy to leave. The close button, X, Escape key and a click outside the menu should all close it. A menu that opens but traps a visitor is not helpful.

**Project and code:** [TRTT GitHub repository](https://github.com/Cocvghuj/Cocvghuj-trtt-news-bot)

## What the menu can coordinate

I call it a front door or a navigation hub because it connects the visitor to the different parts of the project. It does not itself search live fares, issue tickets, book a taxi, activate an eSIM, pay commissions or control third-party platforms. Each destination needs a working service behind it, and each service needs its own checks.

The menu is useful only if its links are accurate, its labels are understandable and the pages behind them provide real help. That is why I am checking the navigation before adding more layers of design.

## Why I am not publishing another flight story today

More posts do not automatically mean a better travel service. A visitor who finds an article but cannot locate the flight, ride, connectivity or passenger-rights resources may leave without getting help. I want the basic journey through the site to make sense before I increase the publishing pace.

Search Console can help me understand indexing, but requesting indexing does not guarantee that Google will index, rank or send traffic to a page. I would rather review the reader experience and publish useful, accurate material than chase a number of URLs. A long article or a page full of affiliate links is not automatically valuable. The content has to answer a traveller’s real question.

## Why flight comparison, passenger rights, rides and eSIMs are included

I have not yet become a direct ticket seller. I am not going to pretend that I have. The practical route I am exploring is to help travellers compare options and reach established providers for parts of their journey.

- **Flight comparison:** a starting point for checking available routes and fares. [Aviasales partner link](https://aviasales.tp.st/Fb8U9yav?marker=787950&sub_id=diary_20261110)
- **Passenger-rights support:** a place to check whether a delayed or cancelled flight may qualify for assistance or a claim. Eligibility depends on the facts and the applicable rules. [AirHelp partner link](https://airhelp.tp.st/tA1pU0Vf?marker=787950&sub_id=diary_20261110)
- **Airport rides:** a way to compare or arrange a transfer rather than leaving ground transport until the last minute. [Kiwitaxi partner link](https://kiwitaxi.tp.st/tIZcbMOB?marker=787950&sub_id=diary_20261110&promo_id=4558)
- **Mobile connectivity:** an eSIM may help a traveller get online without searching for a physical SIM kiosk after landing. Device compatibility and plan coverage must be checked. [Airalo partner link](https://airalo.tp.st/ZZoQC7Mz?marker=787950&sub_id=diary_20261110)

These are routes to providers, not guarantees of the lowest price, successful claims, available seats or a particular travel outcome.

## Why I am not auto-posting every GitHub change

GitHub is useful for keeping code and tracking changes. It is not a reason to turn every code commit into an automatic public article. A change to a menu is a development record, not a flight report. I want to inspect the code, test the behaviour and review the writing before anything is published.

For today, the work is deliberately narrow:
- Put the Thinking Room button inside the header menu.
- Use static positioning, not a fixed floating launcher.
- Keep the eleven agreed menu entries.
- Make close button, X, Escape and outside click work.
- Leave colours, glow, inner redesign and new features untouched.
- Wait for a real screenshot review before saying the live result is correct.

## Why I work with ChatGPT, but keep the decision

I use ChatGPT as a supervised developer assistant to help read code, organise the plan, draft notes and identify possible fixes. I do not want it to invent flight information, auto-publish unchecked pages or claim that a menu works without evidence. AI can help with the work; it cannot replace my review or take responsibility away from me.

## Commercial transparency

Some links in this note are affiliate or partner links. If a reader makes a qualifying booking or purchase, the publisher may receive a commission. This does not guarantee a commission, a discount or a better result for the reader. I will try to explain the purpose of each provider link and encourage travellers to compare current terms before making a decision.

## Closing field note

I am building the route before filling every page with more content. If the Thinking Room menu becomes a reliable front door, it can help a traveller move from flight research to ground transport, connectivity, passenger information and useful travel resources with less searching. That is the reason I have given this menu so much attention. The live result still needs to be checked; this diary does not claim that the menu has passed.
